# The Neighbours

**Scope:** the other honest ways to
keep and hear audiobooks, what each
does better and worse than
Audiobookshelf, and when the answer is
genuinely "use the neighbour."

## A folder and a player app

The zero-server option: files in a
folder, copied to the phone, played by
a stand-alone audiobook player app.
Good ones keep your place, handle
chapters, and cost almost nothing.

- **Better:** nothing to install,
  nothing to run, works on a plane by
  definition.
- **Worse:** every device keeps its
  *own* place; the collection lives
  wherever you copied it; adding a book
  is a cable or a sync ritual.
- **Choose it if:** one listener, one
  device, small shelf.

## Your media server (Plex, Jellyfin)

Both will index audio files, and with
the right plugins and tag discipline
either can be coaxed into presenting
books. Their centre of gravity is
film and music.

- **Better:** already running in many
  houses; one app for everything;
  strong TV and film stories.
- **Worse:** books are guests at
  someone else's table — chapter
  handling, per-book progress, and
  author/series shelves are thinner
  than a purpose-built tool's, and
  podcast support varies.
- **Choose it if:** your books are few
  and your films are many, and one app
  matters more than book-shaped
  shelves.

## A shop's app (Audible and kin)

- **Better:** the catalogue, the
  purchase, the narration quality —
  nothing else is close, and nothing
  else could be.
- **Worse:** the library is rented
  ground. Your place in each book
  lives in the shop's app, on the
  shop's terms; books you own as files
  don't join the shelf; leaving means
  leaving the progress behind.
- **Choose it if:** buying books is
  the activity. Many households run
  this *and* Audiobookshelf — the shop
  for new releases, the server for the
  collection they own outright.

## A podcast app alone

Pocket Casts, AntennaPod, and friends
are excellent at feeds and queues.

- **Better:** discovery, per-show
  settings, and years of podcast-only
  polish.
- **Worse:** heard episodes evaporate
  unless archived by hand; nothing
  keeps a permanent, owned copy;
  books aren't invited.
- **Choose it if:** podcasts are a
  stream you drink from, not a shelf
  you keep. Choose Audiobookshelf's
  podcast side when you want episodes
  *kept* — the show that might vanish,
  the series you're working through at
  your own pace.

## Where Audiobookshelf sits

It is the purpose-built shelf: books
and podcasts as files you own, one
server, every listener's place kept
in one place. It wins when the
collection is the point. It is not a
shop, not a discovery engine, and not
the deepest podcast client — the
neighbours above are each better at
their one thing, and chapter 14 lists
what it doesn't attempt.
